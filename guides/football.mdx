## 2. Storylines Driving Each Sideline

### Oregon: "Wake-up call" mode

- **Slow start is the documented problem.** Boise State scored on its opening 11-play drive, stopped Oregon's first two possessions, and led 17-7. Lanning called the game a wake-up call and said the team "didn't win the critical situations." Expect a scripted, tempo-heavy opening sequence designed to score on the first drive.
- **Situational football failed.** 4-of-12 on third down, 0-of-3 on fourth down, 10 penalties for 90 yards (two unsportsmanlike), a muffed punt that gifted a Boise score, and Boise held the ball for over 31 minutes. Watch fourth-down decision-making and whether the Ducks clean up the penalty discipline on the road.
- **The offense is explosive but not efficient.** 497 total yards at 7.9 yards per play, yet it needed a 62-yard Dakorien Moore catch-and-run with 7:51 left to take the lead for good. The gap between yards and points is the number to track.
- **Motivation.** Oregon heard the public doubt after the drop from No. 2 to No. 6. National-title contenders in that position typically try to make a statement early; look for aggression on early downs and deep shots on the first series.

### Oklahoma State: A rebuilt program that got "punched"

- **Morris's self-assessment.** After Tulsa: "outcoached, outplayed, outphysicaled, outschemed." He publicly questioned his own third- and fourth-down calls (two 4th-and-1 dives stuffed by Tulsa's front four). The Cowboys were 5-of-16 on third down and 2-of-6 on fourth, with three failed fourth-down tries in Tulsa territory.
- **Turnovers killed them.** Four giveaways: two fourth-quarter Mestemaker interceptions (one in the end zone), a Mestemaker lost fumble, and one more. Against Oregon's speed, giveaways become short fields and quick points.
- **The defense was the bright spot.** Held Tulsa to 1-of-11 on third down and forced a pick (LaDainian Fields), but recorded zero sacks, allowed a score in every quarter, and visibly wore down in the fourth.
- **Home opener as identity moment.** First home game under Morris, a 30-game home-opener streak, and the chance to end a 12-game skid against a top-10 team. Expect a maximum-energy first quarter from the crowd and the sideline.

## 4. Key Matchups and What to Watch on Each Snap

- **Oregon EDGE vs. OSU backup left tackle.** Tulsa hit Mestemaker 10 times with a defensive end (Thomas Davis) producing a sack, forced fumble, and four QB hits. Oregon's edge pair is far better. Watch whether OSU chips with a back or tight end, slides protection toward the left, or lives in quick three-step drops to neutralize the rush. If Tuioti or Uiagalelei gets a free run early, the day gets long fast.
- **OSU's short passing game vs. Oregon's tackling.** The realistic OSU game plan is high-percentage quick throws to Young, Hawkins, and Coleman that get the ball out before the rush arrives. Oregon "struggled to break tackles and make plays in space" against Boise and was "bullied for stretches" up front. If OSU strings together 6-to-8-yard completions and Oregon misses tackles, drives sustain and the Boise script repeats.
- **Dante Moore vs. OSU's zero-sack pass rush.** OSU did not sack Tulsa. Oregon's revamped line kept Moore nearly untouched last year and clean vs. Boise. With time, Moore will attack downfield; OSU's counter is to drop seven or eight and bait throws into Fields's zone. Watch for Moore's patience: checkdowns to Johnson and Davison rather than forcing it.
- **Oregon run game vs. Tulsa-style physical front.** Boise matched Oregon at the line of scrimmage; Oregon's early runs went nowhere (2.9 ypc). OSU's front stood up two 4th-and-1 dives. If Oregon cannot run on early downs, it becomes one-dimensional and third-and-long invites OSU's zone drops.
- **Fourth-down and short-yardage execution.** Both teams failed here: Oregon 0-of-3, OSU 2-of-6. Morris is not conservative and will go for it again, likely with more creative calls than the dive. Lanning is aggressive by nature. Count the fourth-down attempts and outcomes; they will decide the margin.
- **Special teams and hidden yards.** Oregon lost the special-teams battle in Week 1 (muffed punt, fair catches, no return game since Gary Bryant Jr. left). Iverson Hooks (Tez Johnson-type slot/returner) is expected back and could take punt-return duties. OSU's best path to an upset is a special-teams score or a short field.

## 5. Formations, Scheme, and Tendencies

### Oregon offense (Will Stein)

- Spread with 11 and 12 personnel; tempo out of the huddle after explosive plays. Heavy use of play-action and RPO tags off zone runs to hit McClellan and D. Moore on deep crossers and go routes.
- First-drive script: expect a shot play within the first three snaps to answer the slow-start narrative. If OSU plays two-high, look for Davison on inside zone and Johnson on the seam.
- Red zone: Johnson (TE) and Davison are the finishers; Oregon's 2025 issue of "yards without points" showed up again vs. Boise, so watch the play-calling from the 20 in.

### Oregon defense (Tosh Lupoi)

- Base 4-2-5 / 3-3-5 hybrid with Uiagalelei and Tuioti as stand-up edges. Lanning's DNA is disguised pressure: simulated blitzes, late safety rotation, and interior twists (Alexander) to pin the QB against the sideline.
- Against a quick-game QB who struggles under pressure, expect press-man on the outside to disrupt timing and early-down blitzes to force third-and-long. Tell: if Oregon shows single-high pre-snap and rotates late, they are baiting Mestemaker into the deep-ball interception he threw twice last week.
- Run defense was "not to our standard" (Lanning) despite 93 yards allowed at 2.6 ypc; watch gap discipline on Hawkins on first down.

### Oklahoma State offense (Eric Morris, Air Raid lineage)

- Air Raid roots (Morris coached under Mike Leach at Texas Tech and ran a pass-first spread at UNT). Expect 10 and 11 personnel, wide splits, mesh and four-verticals concepts, and RB screens.
- Week 1 revealed the weak points: no rhythm, holding penalties, sacks on promising drives, predictable short-yardage. Watch for adjustments: more tempo, more Hawkins, more max-protect, and designed quick throws to Young.
- Turnover risk: Mestemaker forced two picks while trailing in the fourth. If Oregon leads by two-plus scores by halftime, expect desperation throws and takeaway opportunities for Oregon's secondary.

### Oklahoma State defense

- Held Tulsa to 1-of-11 on third down with disciplined coverage and a bend-don't-break profile, but generated zero sacks. Expect soft zone and disguised coverages to stay over the top of McClellan and Moore, conceding underneath throws and runs.
- Fatigue tell: the unit wore down in the fourth quarter vs. Tulsa (Alston's 29-yard TD burst). Watch whether Oregon's tempo produces the same collapse by the third quarter.

### Travel and body clock

- **Oregon:** First road trip of the year, two time zones east, 9 a.m. Pacific kickoff. Among the earliest body-clock starts the Ducks will face all season. Combined with a documented slow-start problem, the first 15 minutes are the upset window. Lanning's program is 18-2 on the road, so the process is proven, but watch for sluggish early execution and false starts on the road.
- **Oklahoma State:** Home, normal 11 a.m. local kickoff, full week to reset after an embarrassing loss. Emotional edge is real but historically fades if the home team falls behind two scores.

### Weather (Stillwater, game-day forecast)

- Sunny at kickoff, around 80°F, climbing toward a high near 89°F with high humidity (low 73°F overnight). Roughly 45% chance of rain showers during the day; check radar before the second half.
- Heat favors the team with depth and rotation: Oregon rotates its defensive line heavily; OSU's defense already faded late vs. Tulsa in cooler conditions. If Oregon runs tempo in the third quarter, expect OSU substitutions and cramping.
- Late-week Pacific Northwest team playing in Oklahoma humidity for the first time is a hydration and conditioning factor. Watch trainers on the Oregon sideline in Q3.

### Endurance and game-flow model

- **Oregon endurance profile:** Strong. Outscored Boise 20-10 after halftime and won the fourth quarter with an explosive play. The Ducks' recent history is winning the second half; the concern is the first quarter, not the fourth.
- **Oklahoma State endurance profile:** Questionable. Ran 86 plays vs. Tulsa (28 first downs) but scored 10 points; the defense spent a lot of time on the field because the offense could not finish drives, and it broke late. A four-turnover team playing from behind in the heat is a recipe for a lopsided fourth quarter.
- **Most likely shape:** Tight or even OSU-leaning first quarter driven by crowd energy and Oregon's slow-start tendency; Oregon separates late in the second quarter as its pass rush and tempo take over; margin widens in the second half as OSU tires and Mestemaker presses. Upset path requires OSU to win the turnover battle by two or more and score on special teams.

## 8. Three Bold Calls to Test Against the Broadcast

- Oregon scores a non-offensive touchdown (defense or special teams). Mixon had a pick-six vs. OSU last year; Hooks or Stewart could spring a return.
- Oregon records five or more sacks. A backup left tackle against Tuioti and Uiagalelei, plus interior push from Alexander, against a QB who holds the ball under pressure.
- Oregon's run game exceeds 175 yards. Lanning wants to "win critical situations"; the cleanest way is a controlled ground game that shortens the game and rests the defense in the heat.
